\section{Prospects}
\label{sec:prospects}

Five extensions are within reach of this machinery, in rough order of
readiness.

\emph{QCD coupled to QED with physical charges.}  The color-contraction
tables already compute both Fierz structures, and the U(1) exchange reuses
the $-1/N$ index structure with different weights.
$H = H_{\rm QCD} + (e^2/g^2)\,H_{\rm QED}$ is therefore a reweighting of
matrix elements already built, and one build serves every charge ratio.
The validation ladder is clean: bit-identity at $e = 0$, the exact
Schwinger mass at the U(1) point, isospin restoration.  One obstruction is
physical.  Gauss' law on a circle admits only neutral states without a
zero-mode subtraction, so the neutron and $\pi^0$ come free and the proton
does not.

\emph{The U($N$) switch.}  The same reweighting in the pure-U(1) limit
exposes the SU($N$)/U($N$) split of Sec.~\ref{sec:massless} numerically.
The thesis~\cite{Hornbostel:1988ne} plots the SU(2)--U(2) comparison the
1990 article never showed.  Reproducing it would close the last
unreproduced published figure of this program.

\emph{Zero modes.}  Our antiperiodic quantization removes the $k^+ = 0$
mode by fiat.  Where zero modes have been included dynamically in
two-dimensional models, low-lying masses shift by up to
$\sim 20\%$~\cite{Mueller:1998tf}.  Their absence is a genuine systematic
of unknown size for our absolute masses.  It is not the cause of the
weak-coupling failure: a continuum solver with no zero-mode truncation
reproduces that failure exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}).
Collective-variable treatments that tensor a zero-mode sector onto the Fock
basis exist and fit our solver architecture.

\emph{Hadronic tensors and finite temperature.}  Light-cone wavefunctions
are raw material for more than spectra.  Structure functions are already in
hand (Sec.~\ref{sec:wavefunctions}), and DLCQ has historically reached
finite-temperature QED$_{1+1}$, including the analogue of the $R$
ratio~\cite{Strauss:2008zx,Strauss:2009zza}.  Two-current correlators and
hadronic tensors are the quantities recent quantum simulations target at
the hundred-qubit scale~\cite{Gustafson:2026ht}.  They are natural DLCQ
observables, computable here with controlled errors at a classical cost of
seconds.  A DLCQ pipeline for $W^{\mu\nu}$ would hand the
quantum-simulation program the reference standard argued for in
Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}.

\emph{An equal-time computation in the same chiral window.}  The sharpest
open discrepancy this paper documents will not be settled by more $K$:
light-front methods find $\alpha \to 1$ where the one equal-time
measurement suggests $\alpha \approx 1.4$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:modern}).  Settling
it needs an equal-time calculation with a controlled additive mass
renormalization at finite $N$, over a decade in $m/g$ rather than a factor
of four.  The missing ingredient is the SU($N$) analogue of the
staggered-fermion mass shift known for the Schwinger
model~\cite{Dempsey:2022nys}.  Deriving it is, in our view, the single most
valuable piece of new work available in this corner of field theory, and
the natural subject of a companion study.
