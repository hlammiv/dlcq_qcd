\section{Approaches measured not to work}
\label{app:donotretry}

Every extrapolation strategy below was implemented and measured against the
standard-Hamiltonian weak-coupling problem of Sec.~\ref{sec:budget} before
being rejected.  We record them because each is the obvious next idea, and
each fails in a way that is invisible from inside the fit --- the same
property that makes the underlying problem dangerous.  Numbers refer to the
Table~\ref{tab:tableone} channels at $m/g \le 0.2$ unless stated.

\begin{description}
\item[More resolution.]  The remainder falls only as $K^{-a}$ with
  $a \to 0$; halving the uncertainty bracket at $m/g = 0.1$ requires
  increasing $K$ by a factor of order $10^{4.9}$, and at the smallest
  couplings we probe, by factors that exceed any conceivable computation.
  Resolution buys nothing below the validity boundary; that is the point.
\item[A confluent fit basis.]  Replacing the near-degenerate
  $\{K^{-1}, K^{-(1+a)}\}$ pair by a confluent basis spans the same space:
  the extrapolated mass is identical to one part in $10^{14}$.
\item[Committing to a single series order.]  Fixing the number of terms
  (say four) hides, not removes, the order dependence: the next admissible
  term still moves the extrapolated mass by several percent.  Two defensible
  selection criteria --- held-out resolution and consistency with the known
  chiral behaviour --- prefer opposite ends of the admissible range, which
  is what an undetermined model order looks like; the budget therefore
  spans the whole ladder rather than choosing.
\item[A validity floor on the fit windows.]  Raising the smallest
  resolution admitted to the window set shrinks the quoted spread severalfold
  while walking the central value monotonically by many times the shrunken
  bar --- variance traded for bias, silently.
\item[Fitting the endpoint exponent freely.]  $M_0 - C\,K^{-p}$ is
  unidentifiable when the true $p$ is small: $K^{-p}$ is nearly constant,
  $M_0$ and $C$ degenerate, and the fit lands an order of magnitude from the
  true value with a residual of $10^{-7}$ --- a perfect fit to the wrong
  answer.
\item[Summing the tail analytically.]  A Hurwitz-$\zeta$ resummation of the
  increment series validates at strong coupling and diverges exactly where
  it is needed.
\item[Adding an explicit $K^{-a}$ column.]  With $a$ fixed from
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent}, the extrapolated masses land several
  times above the published values while the fitted chiral exponent stays
  pinned to the grid artifact; inside a joint $(m/g, K)$ fit the same column
  produces variant spreads of hundreds of percent and negative masses.
\end{description}

The common failure mode deserves the emphasis: every rejected strategy
\emph{improved} some internal fit diagnostic while degrading the answer.
Residuals, held-out predictions, and spread statistics all reward
flexibility inside the reachable window, and the reachable window does not
contain the physics (Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}).  Where we quote weak-coupling
numbers from the standard Hamiltonian, the error bar carries the endpoint
systematic in full; where we need the chiral region, we change the
Hamiltonian, not the fit.
