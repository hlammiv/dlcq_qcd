\section{The chiral region}
\label{sec:chiral}

Table~\ref{tab:tableone} is the 1990 paper's Table~I, redone.  Each of the
\numTableoneNEntries{} entries carries our standard-Hamiltonian value with
the full four-component budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}, our
improved-Hamiltonian value with its ensemble spread, and the published
number with its parenthesis.  The fits use $2K = 25$--$71$, three times the
original resolution range.  Only \numTableoneNOutsidePaperTerm{} of
\numTableoneNEntries{} published entries differ from ours by more than
their own last-term magnitude, which is how the 1990 authors asked to be
scored.  The reproduction, in that sense, is complete.

The two Hamiltonians tell one story in two regimes.  At strong coupling
they agree to a fraction of a percent, and the improved uncertainty is an
order of magnitude smaller.  Below the validity boundary
$m/g \approx 0.15$ they separate.  Measured against fit-only uncertainties,
the worst disagreement reaches
$\numTableoneImprovedWorstFitonlySigma\sigma$.  Measured against the full
budget, endpoint systematic included, no entry disagrees beyond three
standard deviations: \numTableoneImprovedNBeyondThreeSigma{} of
\numTableoneNEntries.  The endpoint term is doing exactly its job.  The
standard weak-coupling numbers are not wrong so much as unreachable, their
bars now say so, and the largest relative bar in the table is
\numTableoneWorstWeakBarPct\% of its central value.

Which of the two columns to believe at weak coupling is not a matter of
taste.  The improved Hamiltonian reproduces van de Sande's exact two-body
value where the exact answer exists, and the standard one does not come
close at any reachable resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:improved}).
