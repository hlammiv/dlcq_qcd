The two Hamiltonians tell one story in two regimes.  At strong coupling
they agree to a fraction of a percent, and the improved uncertainty is an
order of magnitude smaller.  Below the validity boundary
$m/g \approx 0.15$ they separate.  Measured against fit-only uncertainties,
the worst disagreement reaches
$\numTableoneImprovedWorstFitonlySigma\sigma$.  Measured against the full
budget, endpoint systematic included, no entry disagrees beyond three
standard deviations: \numTableoneImprovedNBeyondThreeSigma{} of
\numTableoneNEntries.  The endpoint term is doing exactly its job.  The
standard weak-coupling numbers are not wrong so much as unreachable, their
bars now say so, and the largest relative bar in the table is
\numTableoneWorstWeakBarPct\% of its central value.

Which of the two columns to believe at weak coupling is not a matter of
taste.  The improved Hamiltonian reproduces van de Sande's exact two-body
value where the exact answer exists, and the standard one does not come
close at any reachable resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:improved}).

\subsection{The chiral exponent}
\label{sec:chiral-exponent}

The chiral limit sharpens the comparison into one number.  We fit
$M^2 \propto (m/g)^{\alpha}$ to the $N = 3$ meson over $m/g \le 0.1$ at
fixed resolution, with no extrapolation in $K$ at all.  The physical
exponent is $\alpha = 1$: the mass-squared is linear in the quark mass, by
GMOR and by 't~Hooft's own small-mass expansion.

The standard Hamiltonian gives $\alpha = \numAlphaStandard$, and the value
moves by only \numAlphaStandardDrift{} as $2K$ runs from 30 to 60.  It has
converged, in $K$, to the wrong answer.  The mechanism is
Sec.~\ref{sec:improved}: the grid replaces the endpoint integral by
$\ln K$, which turns $M^2 \sim mg$ into $M^2 \sim m^2 \ln K$, and an
$m^2$ law fits as $\alpha = 2$.  The improved Hamiltonian on the same
couplings gives $\alpha = \numAlphaImproved$, moving by
\numAlphaImprovedDrift{} over the same resolutions.  Stability in $K$ is
what makes this a measurement rather than an artifact of fit choices: no
extrapolation basis enters, and the exponent is a property of the computed
masses alone.

The two-body sector supplies the anchor where an exact answer exists.  At
van de Sande's benchmark coupling the exact ground state is
$M^2/g^2 = \numTwoBodyExact$.  The improved series extrapolates in $1/K$
to \numTwoBodyImproved, a relative agreement of \numTwoBodyImprovedRel;
the same fit on the standard series returns \numTwoBodyStandard.  One
caveat transfers from the meson to the baryon channel.  The improved
self-energy takes the Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent} exponent as input,
and at reachable $K$ the baryon's fitted $\alpha \approx 1$ partly reflects
that input rather than an independent measurement.  We state it here once
and let the baryon numbers carry it.

\subsection{Toward the chiral limit: $m/g$ to $2\times10^{-4}$}
\label{sec:tiny-mg}

Below Table~\ref{tab:tableone}'s smallest coupling the two Hamiltonians
stop describing the same physics.  At $m/g = 1.95\times10^{-4}$, the
smallest coupling probed, the $N=8$ meson gives
$M^2 = \numTinyNeightImprovedMsq$ improved against
$\numTinyNeightStandardMsq$ standard: a factor of \numTinyNeightGap.
The same gap, at the same scale, holds at $N = 5$--$7$.  Nothing in the
standard fits warns of it (Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}).

The gap is not mysterious.  The parameter-free endpoint model of
Appendix~\ref{app:budget}, with its exponent from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent} and no fitted constants, accounts for
ninety-five percent of it across a factor of thirty-two in coupling, with
a residual stable at four to five percent.  The standard Hamiltonian is
measuring the grid; the improved one is measuring the theory.  Its GMOR
behavior supports that reading: against the parameter-free large-$N$ law
the improved masses give $M^2/\mathrm{GMOR} = 1 + 0.71\,(m/g)$ with
intercept $\numGmorIntercept$, an intercept consistent with zero where
the standard Hamiltonian misses the law by a factor of five.

\subsection{The meson--baryon ratio and bosonization}
\label{sec:ratio}

The chiral region carries one finite-$N$ prediction this machinery can
test.  Non-abelian bosonization gives the chiral-limit mass ratio
$M_{\rm mes}/M_{\rm bar} = 2\sin(\pi\nu/2)$ with
$\nu = 1/(2N-1)$~\cite{Date:1986xe}, against the constituent-counting
value $2/N$ that holds at strong coupling.  The two differ by fifteen to
nineteen percent over $N = 5$--$8$.  Sec.~\ref{sec:chiral-exponent}'s
caveat applies with full force here: the meson sector alone cannot carry
finite-$N$ information, since $N$ enters its valence Hamiltonian only
through $C_F$, so the ratio is a baryon measurement.

The improved Hamiltonian lands on bosonization.  At $m/g = 0.0125$ the
ratio sits within \numRatioWorstDevPct\% of $2\sin(\pi\nu/2)$ at
$N = 5, 6, 7$.  The scope of that check is worth stating exactly.  Our
baryon determinations are new and reach $2K = 45$, twelve units deeper
than prior scans; the meson determinations are shared with the companion
analysis, not remeasured.  The confirmation is therefore of the baryon
extrapolation --- the half that carries the large corrections and had no
external anchor --- against a common meson set.  The companion analysis,
with deeper Fock checks, finds the same agreement through $N = 8$ in the
chiral limit, stable from $m/g = 1.6$ down to $2\times10^{-4}$.  The standard Hamiltonian sits on
$2/N$ at every coupling and never departs.  At $N = 2$ the ratio returns
unity to a part in $10^{3}$, forced by pseudo-reality: a free check of the
pipeline, not a result.

Three caveats attach, and we state them at full strength.  The
Fock-convergence residuals across $N = 5$--$8$ drift systematically
through zero near $N = 7$ rather than scattering, so the sub-percent
agreement is not a random error and should not be over-read.  The $N = 8$
baryon rests on fewer momentum points than the others and carries the
least-controlled extrapolation.  And the baryon sector as a whole has
passed exactly one external check, the strong-coupling limit forced by
physics, while carrying the largest corrections anywhere in this paper.
The ratio result is a statement about the improved Hamiltonian's
consistency with bosonization, not yet an independently anchored
measurement of it.
