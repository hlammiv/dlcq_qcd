\section{SU(N) in 1+1 dimensions and the improved Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:sun}

The theory is SU($N$) gauge theory coupled to one fundamental fermion of
mass $m$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} \;=\; -\tfrac14 F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\,\mu\nu}
  \;+\; \bar\psi\,(i\slashed{D} - m)\,\psi ,
  \label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}
in one space and one time dimension.  The coupling $g$ carries dimensions
of mass, so $m/g$ is the only physical parameter.  In the gauge $A^+ = 0$
the gluon carries no dynamical degrees of freedom: a constraint fixes
$A^-$, and integrating it out leaves quarks interacting through an
instantaneous linear potential in $x^-$.  Confinement is kinematic in two
dimensions, in QED$_{1+1}$ as much as here.  Half of the fermion components
are also constrained.  The physical content is one spinless quark field per
color.

The light-cone Hamiltonian takes the form
\begin{equation}
  P^- \;=\; m^2 \sum_{n,c} \frac{a^\dagger_{n c} a_{n c}}{k^+_n}
        \;+\; g^2\, H_I .
  \label{eq:pminus}
\end{equation}
$H_I$ contains the normal-ordered four-quark vertices of the instantaneous
potential, with color structure
$\delta_{c_1 c_3}\delta_{c_2 c_4} - \tfrac1N \delta_{c_1 c_2}\delta_{c_3 c_4}$
and a $1/(\Delta k^+)^2$ kernel.  It also contains a one-body
``self-induced inertia'': the normal-ordering remnant of the exchange,
diagonal in the Fock basis.  Explicit matrix elements are in
Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb}.  Our implementations of them are public
(Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\subsection{The endpoint problem and the improved Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:improved}

Near the endpoints $x \to 0, 1$ the continuum bound-state wavefunction
behaves as $\phi \sim x^{a}$, with the exponent set by the transcendental
condition~\cite{tHooft:1974pnl,Hornbostel:1988fb}
\begin{equation}
  1 - \pi a \cot(\pi a) \;=\; \frac{\pi m^2}{g^2 C_F},
  \qquad C_F = \frac{N^2-1}{2N} .
  \label{eq:endpoint-exponent}
\end{equation}
As $m/g \to 0$ the exponent $a \to 0$, and the endpoint region
$x \lesssim e^{-1/a}$ shrinks below any fixed grid.  A uniform grid with
smallest fraction $1/K$ then replaces the endpoint integral
$\int dx\, x^{2a-1} \sim 1/2a$ by a logarithm $\ln K$.  No reachable
resolution recovers it (Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}).  This is a property of the
grid, not of light-cone quantization: a continuum 't~Hooft solver on a
uniform mesh, sharing no code with the discretized theory, fails the same
way.

Improvement is the standard cure for discretization diseases in lattice
QCD: improved nonrelativistic actions~\cite{Lepage:1992tx} and highly
improved staggered quarks~\cite{Follana:2006rc} both remove the leading
cutoff artifacts at the level of the action rather than the fit.  Van de
Sande's improved Hamiltonian~\cite{vandeSande:1996mc} plays the same role
here and treats the disease at its source.  The self-induced inertia and the Coulomb exchange
carry separate, canceling endpoint singularities.  The improvement replaces
the free-field self-energy by one computed against the interacting endpoint
behavior of Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent}, so the residual kernel
vanishes when either momentum approaches an endpoint.

Within an existing DLCQ code the improvement costs almost nothing, for two
reasons.  First, it is exactly diagonal: swapping the self-energy table
$\sigma_{\rm std} \to \sigma_{\rm imp}$ leaves every interaction kernel,
and the standard path, untouched bit for bit.  Second, the color weight it
needs is $\langle -T_a\!\cdot\!T_c\rangle$ at zero momentum transfer, which
the color-contraction machinery already computes.  The identity
$\sum_{c\neq a}\langle -T_a\!\cdot\!T_c\rangle = C_F$ holds exactly in our
implementation at every $N$ and parton number we use.  We enforce it as a
gate rather than assume it.

One rule attaches to everything that follows.  The two Hamiltonians
approach the continuum along different paths: the improved spectrum
extrapolates in a plain $1/K$ series~\cite{vandeSande:1996mc}, while the
standard spectrum requires the fractional-power ladder of
Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb}, Eq.~(27) there.  No fit in this paper
crosses the two bases.  Sec.~\ref{sec:budget} quantifies what happens when
one does.
