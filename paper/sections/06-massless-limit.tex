\section{Massless mesons and baryons in the $m/g \to 0$ limit}
\label{sec:massless}

At $m = 0$ the spectrum contains towers of exactly massless hadrons, and the
construction --- due to Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb}, Sec.~VI --- survives
unchanged, so we record only what this paper uses it for.  The colour
currents $V^a_k$ built from the quark bilinears obey a Kac--Moody algebra,
and the interacting part of $P^-$ can be rewritten as a quadratic form in
them; states created purely by currents (and, for baryon number, by a
composite baryon operator) are annihilated by it, giving massless mesons,
baryons, and multi-hadron states at \emph{every} resolution $K$, not merely
in the limit.  The valence distribution of the massless baryon is known in
closed form,
\begin{equation}
  q(x) \;=\; N(N-1)\,(1-x)^{N-2},
  \label{eq:massless-sf}
\end{equation}
which at $N = 2$ collapses to the flat meson distribution.  For U($N$) the
extra U(1) current promotes these states to free massive bosons at
$M^2 = g^2/2\pi$ --- the Schwinger mechanism --- which is the sharpest
statement of how SU($N$) and U($N$) differ in two dimensions.

Three later results lean on this section.  The identically-zero last row of
Table~\ref{tab:tableone} is exact and $K$-independent, and our solvers
reproduce it as a structural check at every resolution.  The chiral-region
analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:chiral} uses masslessness as the fixed endpoint
that any $M^2(m/g)$ fit must respect.  And the exotics search of
Sec.~\ref{sec:exotics} inherits a boundary condition: since multi-hadron
states are exactly massless at $m = 0$, every binding energy we measure must
vanish in the chiral limit, and a candidate whose $\Delta$ fails to do so is
diagnosing its own systematics, not new physics.
