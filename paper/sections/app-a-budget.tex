\section{Error-budget definitions}
\label{app:budget}

Every extrapolated mass carries an uncertainty built from four components,
combined in quadrature.  All four are measured.  None is fit.

\begin{description}
\item[\textnormal{\emph{form}}] The spread of the extrapolated mass over
  the order of the fit series: for the standard Hamiltonian, two through
  five terms of the Eq.~(27) ladder of Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb}.  It
  is the dominant fit component, and it is irreducible.  The extrapolate
  does not converge in the order, and the two defensible order-selection
  criteria prefer opposite ends of the admissible range
  (Appendix~\ref{app:donotretry}).  The budget therefore spans the range
  rather than choosing.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{window}}] The spread over contiguous sub-windows of
  the resolution range at fixed order.  It is correlated with \emph{form};
  the ratio of the two runs near one half across the weak-coupling entries.
  The quadrature sum still reproduces the joint order-times-window ensemble
  spread to a few percent at $m/g \le 0.2$, so the budget does not double
  count there.  At strong coupling it mildly over-covers, which we accept.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{trunc}}] The change in the extrapolate when the
  Fock-space truncation is raised by one $q\bar q$ pair.  This component
  grows with $K$ at weak coupling.  It is measured on the analysis window
  actually used, never quoted from a smaller probe.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{endpoint}}] The captured-fraction systematic of the
  grid.  With endpoint exponent $a$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent},
  a grid of resolution $K$ captures a fraction $1 - K^{-2a}$ of the
  endpoint mass integral.  The one-sided upward systematic on $M(0)$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \delta_{\rm ep} \;=\; M(0)\left[\frac{1}{1-K^{-2a}} - 1\right],
    \label{eq:endpoint-budget}
  \end{equation}
  with no free parameters.  It enters the total as an uncertainty, never as
  a correction: the model is too crude to shift central values, and too
  well verified to ignore.  The same expression accounts for ninety-five
  percent of the measured standard-versus-improved gap across a factor of
  thirty in coupling, with a residual stable at four to five percent
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:chiral}).  At $m/g \le 0.2$ it dominates every other
  component and sets the size of the weak-coupling bars in
  Table~\ref{tab:tableone}.
\end{description}

The solver's own floor, at relative $10^{-13}$ from the eigensolver
tolerance, is carried through and never visible at quoted precision.  The
improved Hamiltonian runs the same machinery with the plain-$1/K$ series in
place of the Eq.~(27) ladder.  Its budget has no endpoint component;
removing that error is what the improvement is (Sec.~\ref{sec:improved}).

The scope of the total bar is worth stating in both directions.  It covers
fit-form spread, window spread, Fock truncation, and the endpoint capture
deficit.  It does not cover the choice of the Eq.~(27) basis itself, nor
any statement about the Hamiltonian being the right one; those are checked
by the improved-Hamiltonian column and the external anchors of
Sec.~\ref{sec:modern}, not by the bar.

Two validation habits back the budget rather than extend it.  Held-out
extrapolation fits the low-resolution points and predicts the highest two,
a basis-independent check of the fit form.  And each quoted mass prints the
fraction supplied by extrapolation rather than data, so a reader can see
where the fit interpolates and where it asserts.
