\section{Error-budget definitions}
\label{app:budget}

Every extrapolated mass in this paper carries an uncertainty built from four
components, combined in quadrature.  All four are measured, none is fit.

\begin{description}
\item[\textnormal{\emph{form}}] The spread of the extrapolated mass over the
  order of the fit series --- for the standard Hamiltonian, two through five
  terms of the Eq.~(27) ladder of Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb}.  It is the
  dominant \emph{fit} component and is irreducible at weak coupling: the
  extrapolate does not converge in the order, and the two defensible
  order-selection criteria (held-out resolution; consistency with the known
  chiral behaviour) prefer opposite ends of the admissible range, so the
  budget spans the range rather than choosing an order
  (Appendix~\ref{app:donotretry}).
\item[\textnormal{\emph{window}}] The spread over contiguous sub-windows of
  the resolution range at fixed order.  Correlated with \emph{form} (the
  ratio of the two runs near one half across the weak-coupling entries), but
  the quadrature sum reproduces the joint order-times-window ensemble spread
  to a few percent at $m/g \le 0.2$, so the budget does not double count
  there; at strong coupling it mildly over-covers, which we accept.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{trunc}}] The change in the extrapolate when the
  Fock-space truncation is raised by one $q\bar q$ pair.  This component is
  \emph{not} resolution-independent --- it grows with $K$ at weak coupling
  --- so it is measured on the analysis window actually used, never quoted
  from a smaller probe.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{endpoint}}] The captured-fraction systematic of the
  momentum grid: with wavefunction endpoint exponent $a$ from
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint-exponent}, a grid of resolution $K$ captures a
  fraction $1 - K^{-2a}$ of the endpoint mass integral, so the one-sided
  upward systematic on $M(0)$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \delta_{\rm ep} \;=\; M(0)\left[\frac{1}{1-K^{-2a}} - 1\right],
    \label{eq:endpoint-budget}
  \end{equation}
  with no free parameters.  It is folded into the total as an
  \emph{uncertainty}, never applied as a correction --- the model is too
  crude to shift central values, but too well-verified to ignore: the same
  expression accounts for ninety-five percent of the measured
  standard-versus-improved gap across a factor of thirty in coupling
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:chiral}).  At $m/g \le 0.2$ it dominates every other
  component and is what makes the weak-coupling error bars of
  Table~\ref{tab:tableone} honest.
\end{description}

The solver's own floor (relative $10^{-13}$-level, from the eigensolver
tolerance) is carried through and never visible at quoted precision.  For
the improved Hamiltonian the same machinery runs with the plain-$1/K$ series
in place of the Eq.~(27) ladder, and the endpoint component does not apply
--- removing it is what the improvement \emph{is} (Sec.~\ref{sec:improved}).

Two validation habits back the budget rather than extend it: held-out
extrapolation (fit the low-resolution points, predict the highest two) as a
basis-independent check of the fit form, and the explicit fraction of each
quoted mass supplied by extrapolation rather than data, printed per entry so
a reader can see where the fit is interpolating and where it is asserting.
