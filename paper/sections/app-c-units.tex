\section{Units and conventions}
\label{app:units}

Masses are quoted as $M/g$ unless stated otherwise, and $m/g$ is the quark
mass in units of the coupling.  Our natural combination is $g^2 N/2\pi$;
't~Hooft's classic solution uses $g^2 N/\pi$.  The large-$N$ comparison in
Sec.~\ref{sec:modern} therefore plots $(2\pi/N)^{1/2}\,M/g$ against
$(2\pi/N)^{1/2}\,m/g$, as Fig.~8(b) of Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb} did.
Spectra shown against the coupling use
$\lambda = [1 + \pi m^2/g^2]^{-1/2}$, which sends the free limit to
$\lambda \to 0$ and the chiral limit to $\lambda = 1$, and the
dimensionless mass $M^2/(m^2 + g^2/\pi)$.  Both axes match the 1990
figures.  In code units the resolution is the integer $2K$, whose parity
matches the parton number.  Single-parton momenta are odd integers, so a
structure function lives on $x = k/2K$ with measure $dx = 1/K$.

Two conventions of Ref.~\cite{Hornbostel:1988fb} are traps.  Both bit us,
so we state them plainly.  First, the numbers printed in its Table~I are
$M^2/(m^2+g^2/\pi)$, although the column headers read $M/g$.  The two agree
at no coupling.  We established the identification by recomputing both
quantities at the 1990 parameters and matching digit patterns; our
transcription carries the correction with full provenance.  Every
comparison in this paper converts to genuine $M/g$, and the build
recomputes the comparison in the table's native $M^2$ units as a
cross-check (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

Second, the parenthesized digits in that table are not error bars.  They
record the magnitude of the last term retained in the extrapolation series,
a convergence diagnostic the authors themselves trusted only for
$m/g \gtrsim 0.2$.  Where this paper quotes an uncertainty, it is the full
budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}.  Where it quotes the 1990 numbers, the
parenthesis keeps its original meaning.

Captions in this paper state $N$, $B$, $2K$ or the fit window, $m/g$, the
Fock-space truncation, and the Hamiltonian for every panel.  Where the
original figure left a parameter unstated, we cite the recovered value and
the evidence for it inline.
